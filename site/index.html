What happens when you say “hey TARS”

TARS is a voice assistant for one household. It runs on a Raspberry Pi at home and uses a few cloud services for the hard parts. This page follows one real request from the first word to the follow-up.

TimeAbout 1.4 sfrom your last word to its first sound. It starts answering before you finish, so the work overlaps. BoundaryNothing leaves the houseuntil a second, stricter check is sure it heard “hey TARS”. Until then the cloud lanes stay closed.
The request we follow

Alon Hey TARS, how long do I boil an egg?

TARS Nine minutes for hard, six for soft.

Alon And for a runny one?

The second question needs no wake word.
One request, second by second

0 is the moment you stop talking

Everything left of 0 happens while you are still speaking. That is why the answer comes so fast: by the time you finish, the connections are open, the speaker is known and the reply is already being drafted.

    How it decides it was called

    A fast guess, then a careful check

    Stage 1 is cheap enough to run on every 80 ms of sound, so it is allowed to be a little eager. Stage 2 only runs when stage 1 fires, and it is the one that opens the door.

    It learns from the household

    Every wake teaches the next pair of models

    The generic models start out good on voices they have never heard. The loop makes them good on yours. People only take part in three places, all in the web app.

    1. 1

      Every wake is kept

      Its 3 seconds of audio, the scores and what followed. Close calls, the near-misses, too. All of it stays on the Pi; nothing leaves the house for learning.

    2. 2

      Most label themselves

      What happened next says whether a wake was real.

    3. 3
      People, in the web app

      People answer the rest in Review

      A few taps a day. Each unsure wake has its audio, what TARS heard, what happened, and TARS's guess shaded.

    4. 4
      People, in the web app

      Voices are grouped automatically

      Naming one lets TARS greet that person by name.

    5. 5

      A bigger machine trains a new pair

      A new stage 1 and stage 2, trained on those wakes in about 20 to 25 minutes. Every fifth wake is kept aside for testing, and the new pair is tested end to end against the one in use.

    6. 6
      The gate

      It installs only if it is better for this house and no worse anywhere else

      Better means it answers more of the household's real wakes, or lets fewer through that weren't for TARS. If not, it asks. Once installed, the Pi switches within seconds, and the Models page can switch back.

    Reminders, timers and messages

    It says it again until someone says “got it”

    The same voice that answers can also speak first. You set it by voice or from the web app, and TARS says it when it's due.

    • “Remind me to call the bank at nine.”
    • “Set a pasta timer for twelve minutes.”
    • “Tell Stacey dinner's ready when she's back.”

    Said until it's heard

    TARS says it when it's due, then again every 5 minutes, at most 4 times, until someone says “got it”. After that it's marked Missed.

    due5 min10 min15 minthen Missed

    Timers ring like a kitchen timer

    Until someone turns them off, for at most 15 minutes. TARS can't hear while it makes a sound, so it rings in bursts and listens in between: “stop” works without the wake word.

    “When she's back”

    Waits until TARS next recognises that voice, then says it.

    The Reminders page shows each one and who acknowledged it and how, and sets new ones from a phone.

    When something breaks

    Every stage has a second option

    TARS fails soft, and when it can't recover it tells you in its own voice.

    Stage Normally If that fails
    End of turn Deepgram Flux Local rules
    Speech to text Flux OpenAI transcribes the same recording
    Brain Qwen on Groq Qwen on Cerebras, then OpenAIIf Groq hasn't started answering within half a second, Cerebras is asked too and the first to answer is kept. A service that fails is skipped for the next two minutes, so an outage doesn't cost every turn its timeout; with both out, OpenAI answers.
    Voice Deepgram Aura-2 A spoken error line, made at startup
    Wake models The newest trained pair The pair it was installed with
    Mic or speaker The USB speakerphone TARS exits and the system restarts it

    A failed answer is kept in its conversation, with where it failed, and the web app shows it.

    Under the hood

    Small on purpose

    Two processes, one folder

    The assistant
    The web app, FastAPI and React
    One SQLite database, WAL mode

    The assistant writes while the page reads, on the same machine.

    Every stage is an interface

    TriggerTranscriberBrainVoice

    Swapping a cloud service for a local model is one new class and a config change.

    Every answer is measured

    Each answer is kept with how long each stage took, which model wrote it (Qwen on Groq or Cerebras, OpenAI handed over, the thinking model, or OpenAI as backup) and, if it failed, where and why: speech to text, the answer, or the voice.

    One command checks it all

    voice-assistant --check checks the keys, devices, models, services, the voice and the mic, and says what's wrong and what to do.

    Hardware
