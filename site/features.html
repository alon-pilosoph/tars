What TARS does

Answering a question is the main flow, and How it works follows one. This page is everything else: what each part does, and what it promises.

1Reminders, timers and messages

It says it again until someone says “got it”

The same voice that answers can also speak first. You set it by voice or from the web UI, and TARS says it when it's due.

  • “Remind me to call the bank at nine.”
  • “Set a pasta timer for twelve minutes.”
  • “Tell Stacey dinner's ready when she's back.”

Said until it's heard

TARS says it when it's due, then again every 5 minutes, at most 4 times, until someone says “got it”. After that it's marked Missed.

due5 min10 min15 minthen Missed

Timers ring like a kitchen timer

Until someone turns them off, for at most 15 minutes. TARS can't hear while it makes a sound, so it rings in bursts and listens in between: “stop” works without the wake word.

“When she's back”

Waits until TARS next recognises that voice, then says it.

The Reminders page shows each one and who acknowledged it and how, and sets new ones from a phone.

2The house

Done means the device says so

TARS locks the door, runs the boiler, sets the AC and plays things on the TV, through Home Assistant on the home network. It says something's done only once the device reports it.

Checked, not assumed

After locking, TARS reads the lock back until it says locked. An AC worked by infrared can't report back, so TARS says it sent the command, never that it's on, and takes your word over its guess: asked to turn it off when it thinks it's off, it sends it anyway. A lock that jams is said to have jammed, and an open door is never locked.

Nothing is done by a draft

TARS starts answering before you finish, but a change waits until you have. “Turn off the boiler… no, leave it” leaves it.

Unlocking is for voices it trusts

The door unlocks only for a voice TARS knows and trusts with it, recognised from the request itself, and only when it heard “unlock”. Anyone can lock.

An agent works the TV

For anything that takes several steps, a model works an Android TV box like a person with its remote, reading the screen after every step. It runs in the background, and TARS says how it went after a chime. Devices Home Assistant doesn't work are plugins: a little code of the household's own.

Two requests

Alon Hey TARS, lock the front door.

TARS One moment. Locked.

Alon And play Toy Story on Stremio.

TARS On it.

A minute later, after a chime: “Toy Story is playing.”
3Who's home

The routers already know

TARS knows who's home from whose phone is on the home Wi-Fi. It signs into the routers and asks them every 30 seconds, the way their own web pages do: no app on anyone's phone, and nothing leaves the house.

Home at once, gone only when it's sure

A phone that joins the Wi-Fi is someone home. Someone is out only once no router has seen their phone for ten minutes, while every router answered: a phone asleep on a table drops off for a few. A router that doesn't answer says nothing about anyone, and just after TARS starts nobody is known yet, never “out”.

Reminders follow people

A reminder due for someone who's out goes to their Telegram chat instead of an empty room. A message left “for when she's back” is said a minute after her phone comes home.

Two routers, no official API

An ISP's Technicolor gateway and a Xiaomi access point, each signed into as its own sign-in page does it. The Technicolor's sign-in is SRP, matched byte for byte against its own script run in a browser, and a refused password is left alone for 15 minutes, since the gateway locks out after a few.

Two requests

Alon Hey TARS, who's home?

TARS Just you. Stacey's phone left about two.

Alon Tell her dinner's at eight when she's back.

TARS I'll tell her when she's home.

At 7:12 her phone joins the Wi-Fi; a minute later: “Stacey, a message from Alon…”
4On Telegram

The same TARS, in writing, from anywhere

Each person can chat with TARS on Telegram, in writing or with a voice message: ask it something, set a reminder, have it send something, or have a message said aloud at home. TARS asks Telegram for new messages, so nothing at home is opened to the internet.

Only the people it's told about

It answers the chats named in its settings, each as that person. Anyone else gets no answer at all, so a stranger can't even tell it's there.

Reminders come to where you are

One set in a chat is posted to that chat when it's due. “Tell the house dinner's ready” is said aloud at home instead, and the chat hears when it was said and who said “got it”. Whatever TARS sends at home for someone with a chat comes to their phone as well.

Written back, even to a voice message

A voice message is written down by the same speech to text as at home, and the reply comes in writing. Chats go straight to OpenAI's model: a chat is read whole, so Qwen's faster first word buys nothing there. Each person has their own conversation, kept until the chat has been quiet for an hour, or until they send /new.

A chat, away from home

Alon Remind me to call the bank at nine.

TARS Set for 9 AM. It'll come here.

Alon And tell the house dinner's at eight.

TARS Saying it at home now.

A minute later the chat reads: “Stacey said they got it at 7:42 PM.”
5Errands on the web

Any site, and nothing ordered without a tap

Asked aloud or in a chat, TARS does an errand on a website in its own browser: orders on Wolt, books a table, finds something in an account. Nothing in it is written for any one site. It reads the page the way a screen reader does, clicks and types its way there, and comes back with the answer.

Code presses the last button

The agent may fill a basket and go to the checkout, never place the order. Code checks every click and key first, by what the button says and where it is: a total beside it, a page that takes payment. The person gets the checkout on their phone and taps Order; only then, and only if the amounts are unchanged, is it pressed.

It signs in, and never shows a password

Logins are in TARS's own vault, typed by code only into their own site. One it lacks is asked for on a one-time page; a code the site sends is taken from the reply or the person's email, whichever is first. The model sees none of it, and nothing at home is ever opened, whatever a page asks.

Measured on sixteen live tasks

With the whole errand in view, Luna did every hard task (Wolt, KSP, Booking.com) at about seven cents each; the first prototype did six of sixteen.

Asked aloud, approved on the phone

Alon Order the usual margherita from Wolt.

TARS On it. Approving it comes to your phone.

The checkout comes to the chat with Order and Cancel; once it's placed, TARS says so at home.
6It learns from the household

Every wake teaches the next pair of models

TARS hears its name in two stages on the Pi (How it works). The generic models start out good on voices they have never heard; the loop makes them good on yours. People only take part in three places, all in the web UI.

  1. 1

    Every wake is kept

    Its 3 seconds of audio, the scores and what followed. Close calls, the near-misses, too. All of it stays on the Pi, and in the household's own bucket if the backup is on: none of it is sent anywhere to learn from.

  2. 2

    Most label themselves

    What happened next says whether a wake was real.

  3. 3
    People, in the web UI

    People answer the rest in Review

    A few taps a day. Each unsure wake has its audio, what TARS heard, what happened, and TARS's guess shaded.

  4. 4
    People, in the web UI

    Voices are grouped automatically

    Naming one lets TARS greet that person by name.

  5. 5

    A bigger machine trains a new pair

    A new stage 1 and stage 2, trained on those wakes in about 20 to 25 minutes. Every fifth wake is kept aside for testing, and the new pair is tested end to end against the one in use.

  6. 6
    The gate

    It installs only if it is better for this house and no worse anywhere else

    Better means it answers more of the household's real wakes, or lets fewer through that weren't for TARS. If not, it asks. Once installed, the Pi switches within seconds, and the Models page can switch back.
